Green Campus Audit

A look at the greenery, waste bins, composting, solar power and rainwater harvesting at J D College of Engineering and Management, Nagpur, and how to improve them.

ObjectivesFive areasMethodProblemsSchemesSolutionsImpactConclusion

Objectives

  • Record the greenery, waste bins, composting, solar power and rainwater harvesting on campus.
  • Find the gaps and explain why they matter.
  • Suggest practical improvements the college can carry out.
  • Involve students and staff in looking after the campus.

Who it is for

Students, teaching and non-teaching staff, the college administration, and the maintenance and gardening staff. The NSS unit and any eco-club can lead the follow-up work.

The five areas

What the audit checks in each area.

1 · Greenery

Trees, lawns and gardens

What we check: number and species of trees, lawn and garden area, shade and survival of young saplings.

Why it matters: trees cool the campus, clean the air and shelter birds and insects.

Next step: plant native shade trees, label them and start an adopt-a-tree program.

2 · Dustbins

Waste bins and segregation

What we check: where bins are placed, whether wet and dry waste are kept apart, and whether signs are clear.

Why it matters: mixed waste cannot be composted or recycled and ends up in a landfill.

Next step: colour-coded bins (green for wet, blue for dry) at every block, with signs.

3 · Composting

Garden and canteen waste

What we check: whether leaves, garden waste and food scraps go to a compost pit or vermicompost unit.

Why it matters: compost returns nutrients to the soil and cuts the waste that is dumped or burned.

Next step: set up a compost unit and use the manure on campus plants.

4 · Solar panel

Rooftop solar power

What we check: which roofs have panels, whether they are clean and working, and how much power they supply.

Why it matters: solar power lowers the electricity bill and the college's carbon footprint.

Next step: expand rooftop solar and display how much power it produces.

5 · Rainwater harvesting

Recharge pits and tanks

What we check: roof downpipes, recharge pits, tanks and whether they are clean before the monsoon.

Why it matters: harvesting recharges groundwater and cuts the water wasted as runoff.

Next step: build or repair recharge pits and tanks, and clean them every year.

How we collected data

  1. Walked the whole campus and photographed each of the five areas.
  2. Counted the trees, noted their species, and estimated lawn and garden area.
  3. Interviewed the gardener, maintenance staff and a faculty member.
  4. Compared what we found with what a green campus should have.

Field location

J D College of Engineering and Management, Nagpur, Maharashtra.

Problems we look for

  • Green cover that is small compared with the built-up area.
  • Few native, shade-giving trees, and bare or dusty ground.
  • Saplings that die for lack of watering after plantation drives.
  • Bins that do not separate wet and dry waste.
  • Garden and canteen waste dumped or burned instead of composted.
  • Solar and rainwater systems that are missing or poorly maintained.
  • Low awareness among students and staff.

Community participation

An audit only helps if people keep acting on it. NSS volunteers and an eco-club can run plantation days and adopt-a-tree programs. A student green committee can check the bins, compost unit, solar panels and harvesting system each semester. Alumni can donate saplings.

Expected impact

  • A greener, cooler campus with cleaner air.
  • Waste separated properly, with manure made from garden waste.
  • Lower electricity use from solar power.
  • Better groundwater recharge and less wasted water.
  • A stronger case in NAAC assessment.

Challenges

  • Limited funds and free space.
  • Plants drying out in summer.
  • Habits that are hard to change.
  • Keeping the compost unit and harvesting system maintained.
  • Keeping student interest after the first drive.

Future scope

  • Repeat the audit every year and compare the results.
  • Keep a digital register of trees and estimate the carbon they absorb.
  • Add monitoring for solar output and water use.
  • Aim for green campus certification and share the method with other colleges.

Conclusion

The audit looked at the greenery, waste bins, composting, solar panels and rainwater harvesting of the campus. Planting more native trees, separating and composting waste, expanding solar power and keeping rainwater systems working, with students and staff taking part, can make the campus cleaner, greener and more sustainable.
